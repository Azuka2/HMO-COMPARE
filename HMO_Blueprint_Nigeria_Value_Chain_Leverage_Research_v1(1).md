# HMO BLUEPRINT NIGERIA
# HMO VALUE-CHAIN & STRUCTURAL LEVERAGE INTELLIGENCE
## Parent Groups • Subsidiaries • Insurance • Hospitals • Pharmacies • Diagnostics • Technology • Financing • Distribution • Partnerships

**Research date:** 26 August 2026  
**Purpose:** Add a structural/value-chain layer to the HMO Intelligence Platform so that HMOs are not compared only on visible premiums and benefits, but also on the assets, group relationships, technology, distribution and operating capabilities that may give them a legitimate advantage.

---

# 1. WHY VALUE-CHAIN INTELLIGENCE MATTERS

An HMO does not operate in isolation.

Two HMOs can publish similar benefits and charge similar premiums but have very different underlying ecosystems.

One may have:

- a pharmacy network
- a diagnostic business
- hospitals
- insurance companies
- pension businesses
- asset-management capability
- a technology platform
- payment infrastructure
- a large corporate distribution network
- a parent company with significant capital
- international healthcare relationships

Another may be a standalone HMO dependent almost entirely on third parties.

Neither structure automatically makes one better.

But the structure can create **potential leverage**.

The platform therefore needs a new analytical question:

> **“What does this HMO control, own, access or strategically partner with that could improve its ability to deliver healthcare, manage cost, acquire customers, process claims, negotiate provider rates, manage data or scale?”**

This should be called:

# HMO STRUCTURAL LEVERAGE

Do not confuse structural leverage with proven service quality.

A parent company may provide capabilities.

The HMO still needs to demonstrate that those capabilities actually improve outcomes, price, service or customer experience.

```text
        ↓

        ↓

        ↓
DIAGNOSTICS
        ↓

        ↓

        ↓
QUALITY ASSURANCE
        ↓
WELLNESS / PREVENTION
        ↓
DATA / ANALYTICS
        ↓
RENEWAL / RETENTION
```

Around the chain, map:

```text

PHARMACY
DIAGNOSTICS
HOSPITALS
TECHNOLOGY
```

The platform should identify which parts are:

- Owned

- Publicly claimed
- Independently verified
- Unknown

# 20. OWNERSHIP TYPE

For every relationship classify:

### DIRECT OWNERSHIP

HMO owns the asset.

### PARENT OWNERSHIP

Parent group owns both.

### SISTER COMPANY

Same parent.

### MINORITY INVESTMENT

HMO/group owns a minority interest.

### STRATEGIC PARTNER

Formal partnership.

### CONTRACTUAL NETWORK

No ownership.

### PUBLIC CLAIM

Company says relationship exists.

### UNVERIFIED

No adequate evidence.

This avoids false claims of vertical integration.

# 22. VERTICAL-INTEGRATION RISK FLAG

Vertical integration can create potential advantages but also possible conflicts.

Research:

- Does the HMO steer patients toward affiliated providers?
- Is there independent provider choice?
- Are affiliated providers treated differently?
- Are prices transparent?
- Is the patient able to choose outside the group where the plan permits?
- Does affiliation affect authorization?
- Is quality objectively monitored?

Do not assume conflict exists.

Flag for research.

# 30. HOW THIS CHANGES HMO RANKING

Structural leverage should NOT dominate the overall ranking.

Recommended principle:

```text
Actual Customer Value
        >
Structural Capability
        >
Marketing Claim
```

Example:

An HMO owning a diagnostic center receives:

### Structural leverage:
Yes.

But if there is no evidence that members receive:

- faster diagnostics
- lower prices
- better access
- better quality

then:

### Demonstrated member benefit:
Unknown.

This prevents conglomerate size from becoming a proxy for healthcare quality.

# 32. NEW “WHY THIS MATTERS” PANEL

Every structural relationship should have a consumer translation.

Example:

### Affiliated pharmacy network

Potential relevance:

> May improve medicine distribution or access, but we have not verified that members consistently receive lower prices or faster fulfillment.

### Insurance parent

Potential relevance:

> May provide corporate distribution, insurance expertise and broader financial infrastructure. This does not by itself prove better healthcare.

### Owned diagnostics

Potential relevance:

> Could support coordination and turnaround time. Verify whether the exact plan gives preferential access.

# 33. NEW “SUPPLY-CHAIN MOAT” CONCEPT

For each HMO calculate:

# SUPPLY-CHAIN MOAT

Measures how much of the healthcare delivery chain the group can influence.

Possible layers:

1. Financing
2. Customer acquisition
3. HMO administration
4. Primary care
5. Hospitals
6. Pharmacy
7. Diagnostics
8. Technology
9. Wellness
10. International referral

A diversified group could have:

**High structural moat**

But that does not automatically mean:

**High customer value.**

Therefore always display both.

# 34. NEW “DEPENDENCY RISK” SCORE

The opposite of vertical integration.

Measure reliance on:

- external pharmacies
- external diagnostics
- third-party hospitals
- third-party technology
- external claims systems
- external telemedicine

High external dependency is not necessarily bad.

But it can create:

- coordination risk
- negotiation risk
- service inconsistency
- data fragmentation

The score should be contextual.

# 35. FINAL PRINCIPLE

The HMO Blueprint engine should ultimately be able to say:

> **HMO A has the strongest structural healthcare ecosystem.**
>
> But:
>
> **HMO B gives YOU the better plan.**

That distinction is vital.

A company may own:

- hospitals
- pharmacies
- diagnostics
- insurance
- technology

and still provide a plan that is not suitable for the user's needs.

Therefore:

# STRUCTURAL POWER ≠ CUSTOMER VALUE

The platform must analyze both.

> **Who has the strongest ecosystem?**
